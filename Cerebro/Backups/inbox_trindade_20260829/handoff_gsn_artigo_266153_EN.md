**By Global South News**

Marjorie Taylor Greene’s claim that Donald Trump’s circle has discussed the use of nuclear weapons against Iran in strategy meetings has pushed one of the most dangerous questions in world politics into the open: how far is Washington prepared to go to protect its position in West Asia and its ally Israel?

On *Dialogue Works*, Nima R. Alkhorshid treated the allegation not as routine campaign noise, but as a warning sign from inside a political system that has already normalized surprise attacks, sanctions, threats and permanent military pressure against Iran.

Quoting the substance of Greene’s post on X, Alkhorshid said: "They are discussing using nuclear weapons on Iran in strategy meetings. And yes, you read that correctly. It's real. It's not. I'm not speculating. I know. And it's pure evil."

The remark lands in the middle of the 2026 electoral season, but its meaning goes far beyond domestic politics. It points to a strategic crisis in Washington: the inability to bend Iran through conventional pressure, combined with the enduring demand to maintain Israel’s regional military superiority at almost any cost.

According to Alkhorshid, the United States has already launched two surprise attacks against Iran, while facing shortages in both offensive and defensive missiles. In that context, he argued, the nuclear discussion reflects not strength, but desperation. A power unable to impose its will by conventional means begins to flirt with the unthinkable.

Iran, in his account, is not the fragile target imagined by war planners. It is a state with deep strategic patience, a long historical memory and the ability to strike back across vast distances. Alkhorshid stressed that Iran has had the technical capacity to produce nuclear weapons for many years but has chosen not to do so, while retaining enriched uranium and a serious deterrent capability.

He pointed in particular to missile range. "Iran has the capabilities to hit the American base in Australia. That American base is Australian. American base is something from Iran to Pine Gap is the distance is 11,000 kilometers. So the distance between Iran and Washington is less than that. It's something like 10,000 kilometers," Alkhorshid said.

That calculation matters because it undermines the old imperial assumption that wars can be fought far away, on other people’s land, while the centers of power remain insulated from consequence. The geography of retaliation has changed. Bases, ports, logistics hubs and political capitals are no longer abstract dots on a planner’s map.

The most explosive part of Alkhorshid’s analysis concerned Israel. He argued that US-supplied air defense systems, including THAAD and Patriot batteries, have failed to provide the shield promised by Washington. In his words, Israel is exposed in a way that its leaders and supporters are reluctant to admit.

"The fact is that Israel is naked today. And any attack on Israel, they're going to hit the target and easily hit the target," he said.

Alkhorshid went further, warning that any nuclear strike on Iran would not save Israel, but place it in existential danger. "If Iran decides to hit Israel, they're going to level Israel. There would be nothing left of Israel," he said.

The point is not theatrical escalation. It is deterrence. For decades, Washington and Israel have relied on the idea that overwhelming force can compel regional obedience. Iran’s posture challenges that equation. A nuclear attack would not produce submission. It would trigger a regional catastrophe and a global political rupture.

Alkhorshid emphasized that the use of nuclear weapons would transform the international system irreversibly. "The use of nuclear weapon is far more devastating. The outcome of what would happen in the aftermath of that would be devastating for the United States in my opinion," he said.

The strategic implications are severe. If a nuclear power used such weapons against a non-Western state under the banner of regional security, the message to the rest of the world would be unmistakable: sovereignty is conditional, treaties are fragile, and survival requires the ultimate deterrent. The result would likely be a rush by states everywhere to seek nuclear protection of their own.

This is why the discussion is not only about Iran. It is about the entire architecture of global power. A nuclear attack would expose the rules-based language of the West as a mask for hierarchy, in which one bloc reserves for itself the right to decide which nations may defend themselves and which must live under permanent threat.

Alkhorshid’s final warning was directed at the illusion that Iran can be frightened into surrender. "These people don't capitulate. They fight till the last moment. And if the whole agenda of the United States in the region, the West Asia, is to save Israel or to help Israel, this is not the way that you help Israel," he said.

That sentence captures the central contradiction of Washington’s approach. In trying to preserve Israeli dominance through escalation, it may be accelerating the very danger it claims to prevent. In trying to intimidate Iran, it may be strengthening the logic of resistance across the region.

The revelation attributed to Greene should therefore be treated with the gravity it deserves. Nuclear threats are not campaign rhetoric. They are a sign that the most militarized faction of US politics is prepared to gamble with human civilization when confronted by a sovereign country that refuses to capitulate.

For the Global South, the lesson is stark. Multipolarity is not an academic concept. It is the struggle to build a world in which no single power can threaten nuclear annihilation because another nation refuses to obey.
