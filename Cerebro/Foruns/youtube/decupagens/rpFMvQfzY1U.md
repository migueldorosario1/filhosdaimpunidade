## Texto corrigido

They are here in the center of the Spanish enclave of Bolivia, where thousands and thousands enter every single street in the city center. Enough is enough. They want the Spanish government to deal with the crisis that has been ongoing here since more than 70,000 migrants crossed the border from Molotov just over a month ago. Now, the majority of them left, but there are still, by the Spanish government's estimate, at least 5,000 migrants here. By local authorities' estimates, as many as 9,000. And in this small community of 84,000 people, it is having a massive impact. There have been huge issues and tensions. Migrants have been sleeping in makeshift camps, on the streets, on the beaches. And people here are saying enough is enough. They're fed up. They can't handle it anymore. We've been hearing a lot of different chants here tonight. Even though this is not supposed to be an explicitly political event, it has quickly turned into one. People saying, Pedro Sanchez, the prime minister, should go to prison. People saying, the invaders, referring to the migrants who have come here, must be kicked out. People saying that Ceuta must defend itself, that it is not for sale. And just, you really have a sense of a huge amount of frustration and a sense of abandonment. Meanwhile, the situation for those migrants is incredibly difficult, incredibly tense. Just last week, a large group of protesters went to one of those camps and set fire to them. So clearly, everybody on all sides of this complex situation is angry, is frustrated, and is scared.
